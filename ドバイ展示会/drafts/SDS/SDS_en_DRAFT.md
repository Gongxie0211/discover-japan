## SECTION 4: FIRST AID MEASURES

### 4.1 Description of First Aid Measures

**Inhalation:**
Remove the affected person to fresh air immediately. If the person is not breathing, apply artificial respiration and call emergency services. If breathing is difficult, administer oxygen by trained personnel. If symptoms persist (coughing, throat irritation, shortness of breath), seek medical attention. Note: thermal decomposition may produce HF vapors — see Section 2.3.

**Skin contact:**
Remove contaminated clothing immediately. Wash affected skin with large amounts of soap and water for at least 15 minutes. If skin irritation develops or persists, seek medical attention. Wash contaminated clothing before reuse.

**Eye contact:**
Immediately flush eyes with a directed stream of clean water for at least 15 minutes, holding eyelids open. Remove contact lenses if present and easy to do after initial flushing. If irritation, redness, or pain persists, seek immediate medical attention.

**Ingestion:**
Do not induce vomiting. Rinse mouth with water. Give one or two glasses of water or milk to drink if the person is conscious and able to swallow. Do not give anything by mouth to an unconscious person. Seek immediate medical attention. Show this SDS or product label to attending medical personnel.

### 4.2 Most Important Symptoms and Effects, Both Acute and Delayed

Based on the product's composition and GHS classification:

- **Acute:** Minor skin or eye irritation possible upon prolonged or repeated contact. Ingestion may cause gastrointestinal discomfort.
- **Delayed:** No chronic health effects expected under normal conditions of use. See Note A in Section 3 regarding silica form.
- **Thermal decomposition products (HF):** Exposure to hydrogen fluoride can cause severe burns to skin, eyes, and mucous membranes, and systemic toxicity. Symptoms may be delayed.

### 4.3 Indication of Immediate Medical Attention and Special Treatment Needed

In case of ingestion or significant exposure, inform medical personnel that the product contains fluoropolymer and fluorinated compounds. In cases of suspected HF exposure (e.g., after fire or incineration), treat as HF poisoning and seek specialist toxicological advice. Contact the local Poison Control Center for guidance.

## SECTION 5: FIRE-FIGHTING MEASURES

### 5.1 Extinguishing Media

**Suitable extinguishing media:** Water spray, dry chemical powder, CO₂, foam. This product is water-based and non-flammable.
**Unsuitable extinguishing media:** None identified. High-pressure water jets may disperse product.

### 5.2 Special Hazards Arising from the Substance or Mixture

This product is **non-combustible** under normal conditions.

Upon exposure to fire or extreme heat (>300°C), thermal decomposition may produce:
- Hydrogen fluoride (HF) — acutely toxic, corrosive
- Carbon monoxide (CO) and carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- Other organic fluorinated fumes

**Do not incinerate containers.** Hazardous fumes from decomposition of fluorinated components may be released.

### 5.3 Advice for Fire-fighters

Wear self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) and full protective gear when fighting fires involving this product. Cool containers exposed to heat with water spray. Collect fire-fighting water to prevent environmental contamination — do not allow to enter drains or water bodies.

## SECTION 6: ACCIDENTAL RELEASE MEASURES

### 6.1 Personal Precautions, Protective Equipment and Emergency Procedures

**For non-emergency personnel:** Keep unnecessary personnel away from the spill area. Ensure adequate ventilation. Avoid contact with skin and eyes. Use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) as described in Section 8.

**For emergency responders:** In addition to the above, wear SCBA if ventilation is inadequate or if product has been involved in a fire. Identify and eliminate ignition sources (product is non-flammable, but decomposition products may be hazardous).

### 6.2 Environmental Precautions

Prevent large spills from entering drains, sewers, waterways, or soil. If the product enters a drain or waterway, notify local authorities. Although the product is not classified as environmentally hazardous, large quantities entering aquatic environments should be reported.

### 6.3 Methods and Materials for Containment and Cleaning Up

**Small spills:** Contain using inert absorbent material (sand, vermiculite, dry earth, or paper towels). Collect absorbed material and place in an appropriate closed container for disposal. Wash the spill area with water.

**Large spills:** Contain and collect with non-combustible absorbent material. Prevent runoff to drains. Collect in sealed containers for disposal in accordance with local regulations (Section 13).

**Do not:** Use sawdust or other combustible absorbent materials. Do not flush to sewer without local authority approval.

### 6.4 Reference to Other Sections

See Section 8 (exposure controls/PPE), Section 13 (disposal considerations), Section 15 (regulatory information).

Stable under recommended storage conditions (5–35°C, away from freezing, protected from direct sunlight). Product performance may be impaired by freezing — see Section 7.2.

No hazardous reactions under normal conditions. Thermal decomposition above 300°C produces HF and organic fluorinated fumes (see Sections 2.3 and 5.2).
